# East Carroll Parish Trails Plan: Hiking, Biking, and Rail-Trail

Prepared 2026-10-29. Companion to `FUNDING_ROADMAP.md`. The goal is simple: get people outside and moving. This document picks the corridors, ranks them, and maps each to the money that can build it.

## 1. What exists today

From the QGIS project (`gis/east_carroll_parks.qgz`, layers `trails_*`, `rail_corridors`, `levees`):

| Corridor type | Inside the parish | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Built footway or path | 0.9 km | Essentially nothing. A few sidewalks in Lake Providence tagged as footway. |
| Named unpaved tracks | 22 km | Brushy Bayou Trail (4.6 km), Levee Road (3.2 km), parish roads. |
| Unnamed farm and levee tracks | 249 km | Private farm roads and levee crown roads. |
| Rail line (all disused) | 54.9 km | Ex-Missouri Pacific line running the full length of the parish, north to McGehee AR and south to Tallulah. |
| Mississippi River main-line levee | 47.9 km | Continuous levee crown road along the east edge of the parish. |

The parish has strong raw material and almost no finished product. That is the pitch to every funder: small dollars convert existing public corridors into usable trail.

## 2. Candidate corridors, ranked

### A. Lake Providence Lakeshore Loop (walk and bike, 1 to 3 years)
- **What:** A paved 8 to 10 foot shared-use path along the Lake Providence shoreline, connecting the downtown historic district, the Louisiana State Cotton Museum, the existing ball fields and tennis courts, the two boat ramps, and the schools on the north side. Phase 1 is a 2 to 3 mile out-and-back on the town side of the lake.
- **Why first:** Entirely within town limits on public or already-recreational land, no railroad or Corps of Engineers permission needed, serves the two tracts with 37 to 40 percent poverty, and gives every later application a visible success.
- **Funding:** Recreational Trails Program (80/20), LWCF (50/50), Louisiana DOTD Transportation Alternatives Program (80/20, $20 million statewide per call), AARP Community Challenge for benches and fitness stations, LDWF Boating and Fishing Access for the ramp and pier segments.

### B. Mississippi River Levee Trail (bike, 1 to 2 years for a pilot)
- **What:** Designate and sign the levee crown road from the Lake Providence area north and south as a gravel bike route, with a trailhead and parking at one or two levee access points. Long-term, pave a segment nearest town.
- **Why:** The levee road already exists and already carries occasional traffic. Levee-top trails are the signature bike facility in Baton Rouge, New Orleans, and Vicksburg. The Mississippi River Trail (US Bicycle Route 45) follows US 65 through the parish, so a levee alternative pulls touring cyclists off the highway shoulder and into town.
- **Permissions:** The Fifth Louisiana Levee District controls the levee; Louisiana law (R.S. 38:301 series) lets a levee board allow public recreational use when structural integrity is maintained. Any paving or structures also need Army Corps of Engineers Section 408 review through the Vicksburg District. Start with signed gravel use, which usually needs only the levee board's agreement.
- **Funding:** Recreational Trails Program, DOTD TAP, Louisiana Outdoors Forever as match, Rails-to-Trails Conservancy trail grants (small, next cycle spring 2027), Delta Regional Authority SEDAP as tourism infrastructure.

### C. Rail-Trail or Rail-with-Trail on the Lake Providence Line (3 to 10 years)
- **What:** The ex-Missouri Pacific line runs 55 km the full north-south length of the parish, and every one of the parish's 8 block groups (all 7,195 residents) lies within one mile of it through Lake Providence, Transylvania, and Alsatia. Every segment is tagged disused in OpenStreetMap, and news coverage describes the tracks south of Lake Providence as abandoned in practice.
- **Legal status (this is the hard part):**
  - The line is owned by Delta Southern Railroad, acquired by Patriot Rail in 2022. It is not formally abandoned at the Surface Transportation Board (STB). A line that is out of service but still legally a railroad cannot be converted to a trail without the railroad's agreement.
  - The Lake Providence Port Commission filed an STB feeder-line application (docket FD 36443) in 2020 to buy the 20-mile segment between mileposts 471 and 491 so the North Louisiana & Arkansas Railroad could restore service. Delta Southern sued in state court; the Second Circuit ruled on February 26, 2025 that the Port Commission's authority stops at the parish line and it could not pursue the purchase. The STB still listed the proceeding as pending in 2025.
  - **Implication:** The parish's economic development interests want the railroad running, not removed. The realistic path is **rail-with-trail**: a trail built inside or beside the right-of-way under a license from the railroad, which many short lines grant where the line is dormant. If Delta Southern ever files for abandonment, the parish should immediately request **railbanking** under 16 U.S.C. 1247(d), which preserves the corridor for future rail while allowing interim trail use.
- **Action now:** Send a letter to Patriot Rail asking for a rail-with-trail license on the 3 to 4 mile stretch through Lake Providence. Ask the Northeast Louisiana Railroad Development District and the Port Commission to endorse it so the trail is framed as compatible with rail restoration. Monitor STB docket FD 36443 and any abandonment filing.
- **Funding:** Rails-to-Trails Conservancy (planning grants, technical help), DOTD TAP, RTP, LWCF, DRA.

### D. Brushy Bayou Trail and Bayou Macon WMA Connector (hike, 2 to 4 years)
- **What:** Brushy Bayou Trail already exists as a 4.6 km named track. Formalize it with a trailhead, blazes, and a short loop, and plan a connector toward Bayou Macon Wildlife Management Area on the western edge of the parish for hiking and birding.
- **Permissions:** LDWF manages the WMA; trail work there goes through LDWF. Brushy Bayou ownership must be confirmed from parish assessor records.
- **Funding:** RTP (non-motorized), Louisiana Outdoors Forever, LDWF, NPS RTCA planning assistance, eBird and Audubon partnerships for birding trail designation.

### E. Safe Routes in Lake Providence (walk, 1 year)
- **What:** Sidewalk and crossing fixes linking the schools (General Trass High, Griffin-Southside Elementary, Northside Elementary) to the lakeshore loop and the ball fields.
- **Funding:** DOTD TAP includes Safe Routes to School. Low cost, fast, and it feeds daily use of the loop.

## 3. Funding specific to trails

| Program | Agency | Share | Typical award | Next deadline | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recreational Trails Program | LA Office of State Parks | 80% | $50k to $200k | July 1, 2027 | A, B, D |
| Transportation Alternatives Program (TAP) | LA DOTD | 80% | $200k to $1.5M | Next call expected early 2027 (2025 call ran Feb to Mar) | A, B, C, E |
| LWCF | LA Office of State Parks | 50% | $100k to $500k | April 1, 2027 | A, D |
| Louisiana Outdoors Forever | LDWF | match source | $100k to $1M | 2028 cycle pre-app ~July 2027 | B, D |
| Rails-to-Trails Conservancy Trail Grants | RTC | 100% | $5k to $25k | Spring 2027 (2026 closed May 10) | B, C |
| PeopleForBikes Community Grant | PeopleForBikes | 100% | $5k to $10k | Check peopleforbikes.org/grants | A, B |
| AARP Community Challenge | AARP | 100% | $5k to $25k | ~March 2027 | A, E |
| DRA SEDAP | Delta Regional Authority | varies | $150k to $1M | ~July 2027 | B, C |
| NPS RTCA | National Park Service | staff time | 0 | ~March 2027 | all (planning) |

TAP is the biggest single pot. It reimburses rather than grants, so the parish needs cash flow to front costs. Both TAP and RTP require SAM.gov registration.

## 4. Sequence

1. **Winter 2026 to 2027:** Create the recreation district. Register SAM.gov. Apply to NPS RTCA for a parish trails master plan. Hold one public meeting on the lakeshore loop and levee trail. Send the rail-with-trail letter to Patriot Rail.
2. **Spring 2027:** TAP application for the lakeshore loop phase 1. LWCF application for the trailhead park at the ball fields. AARP and RTC small grants for benches, signage, a bike repair station.
3. **Summer 2027:** RTP application for levee trail signage and trailhead. Louisiana Outdoors Forever pre-application for match.
4. **2028:** Build phase 1. Open the levee route. Begin rail-with-trail design if the license is granted.
