# East Carroll Parish Parks & Recreation: Federal and State Funding Roadmap

Prepared 2026-10-29. Verify every deadline against the agency page before relying on it; many cycles were confirmed only from older notices.

## 1. Why East Carroll is competitive

Figures below are from the Census Bureau's 2023 American Community Survey 5-year estimates (summary file tables B01003, B17021, B19013), stored in `gis/data/processed/acs_2023_east_carroll.json`.

| Geography | Population | Median household income | Poverty rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| East Carroll Parish | 7,195 | $28,321 | 35.7% |
| Tract 1 (north, Lake Providence area) | 2,104 | $28,100 | 36.7% |
| Tract 2 (central) | 2,743 | $32,188 | 22.9% |
| Tract 3 (Lake Providence south side) | 2,348 | $26,198 | 40.3% |

What that means for applications:

- **Every tract qualifies as low-to-moderate income** for Louisiana Community Development Block Grant (LCDBG) purposes, so parish-wide projects meet the national objective without a survey.
- **The parish is a USDA persistent-poverty county**, which unlocks the USDA Community Facilities Economic Impact Initiative and raises the grant share on Community Facilities awards.
- **The parish is inside the Delta Regional Authority (DRA) footprint** and is classified as distressed, which raises the DRA share and lowers match.
- **Lake Providence, the Mississippi River frontage, Bayou Macon Wildlife Management Area, and the Louisiana State Cotton Museum** give the parish real outdoor-recreation and heritage-tourism assets to build around.

## 2. Step zero: build the entity that can receive money (first 60 days)

Most federal and state programs pay only political subdivisions. Do these in order.

1. **Decide the applicant entity.** Options are the East Carroll Parish Police Jury itself, the Town of Lake Providence, or a new Recreation District created by the Police Jury under La. R.S. 33:4562. A recreation district can levy a dedicated millage and hold property, which strengthens long-term maintenance commitments that LWCF reviewers look for. Recommended: Police Jury adopts an ordinance creating "East Carroll Parish Recreation District No. 1" covering the whole parish, with a five-member board.
2. **Get a Unique Entity ID and an active SAM.gov registration** for the applicant. Free, but takes 2 to 6 weeks. Nothing federal moves without it. Register at sam.gov.
3. **Register in Grants.gov** and in Louisiana's LaGov / Office of Community Development portals.
4. **Adopt a simple Parks and Recreation Master Plan** (10 to 20 pages). LWCF scoring in Louisiana is tied to the 2020 Statewide Comprehensive Outdoor Recreation Plan (SCORP) priorities. A plan that cites SCORP priorities and includes the maps in this repo will score higher. The National Park Service RTCA program (see 4.6) can help write it at no cost.
5. **Inventory and title-check parcels** the parish or town already owns that could host facilities. LWCF requires the sponsor to hold title or a long-term lease and to keep the site in recreation use in perpetuity.
6. **Line up match.** Most programs below require 20 to 50 percent. Sources of match: parish or town general fund, Louisiana Outdoors Forever (designed specifically as match money), DRA funds, donated land or in-kind labor, and capital outlay from the legislature.
7. **Open a line to the Northeast Louisiana planning partners.** The North Delta Regional Planning and Development District (northdelta.org) is the DRA Local Development District for this parish and writes LCDBG and DRA applications for member parishes.

## 3. Priority funding programs for parks and recreation

Ranked by fit and probability of award for a first-time rural applicant.

### 3.1 Louisiana Community Development Block Grant, Public Facilities (state-administered HUD money)
- **Administered by:** Louisiana Office of Community Development, Local Government Assistance (doa.la.gov/doa/ocd-lga).
- **What it funds:** Public facilities including community centers and recreation facilities serving LMI residents. About $11 million statewide per cycle.
- **Match:** Minimal. Distressed parishes often qualify for 0 to 5 percent.
- **Timing:** Two-year cycle (FY 2026/2027). Phase I project severity packages were due in 2026; invitations for full applications were expected around August 2026. The FY 2027 cycle information was to be posted August 2026. Each applicant may submit one application.
- **Action:** Confirm with OCD whether East Carroll has an open LCDBG application already. If not, prepare for the next cycle and hold the required public needs hearing.

### 3.2 Land and Water Conservation Fund (LWCF), state side
- **Administered by:** Louisiana Office of State Parks, Division of Outdoor Recreation (lastateparks.com/grant-opportunities-for-outdoor-recreation/land-water-conservation). Toll-free 1-888-677-1400.
- **What it funds:** Acquisition and development or renovation of outdoor recreation facilities: ball fields, playgrounds, trails, boat launches, splash pads, pavilions, lighting.
- **Match:** 50 percent. Louisiana Outdoors Forever money can be used as match.
- **Deadline:** Historically April 1 each year. Confirm 2027 date.
- **Action:** Pick one shovel-ready site (the existing ball field and tennis complex in Lake Providence is the obvious candidate for a renovation request). Get the State Historic Preservation Office clearance letter early; it is a required attachment.

### 3.3 Recreational Trails Program (federal highway money through State Parks)
- **Administered by:** Office of State Parks. Coordinator Michael Domingue, mdomingue@crt.la.gov.
- **What it funds:** Trail construction and maintenance, trailheads, signage, water-trail access. About $1.5 million statewide per year. 80/20 reimbursable.
- **Deadline:** Historically July 1. The 2027 application packet was posted in June 2026.
- **Action:** A lakeshore walking and biking loop around Lake Providence, or a paddle trail with launches on Lake Providence and Bayou Macon, fits this program exactly.
- **Existing corridor inventory** (OpenStreetMap linework from the Take-a-Hike repo, clipped to the parish, layers `trails_named`, `trails_paths`, `trails_tracks` in the GeoPackage): the parish has essentially no built recreational trail today. Only 0.9 km of footway and 22 km of named unpaved tracks exist, and about 249 km of unnamed farm and levee tracks. The two named corridors with trail potential are Brushy Bayou Trail (4.6 km) and Levee Road (3.2 km) along the Mississippi River levee. An RTP application that converts the levee road into a signed levee-top trail, or formalizes Brushy Bayou Trail with a trailhead, uses existing public right-of-way and needs little land acquisition.

### 3.4 Louisiana Outdoors Forever
- **Administered by:** Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries, LAOutdoorsForever@la.gov.
- **What it funds:** Conservation and outdoor access projects; explicitly intended as match to leverage other grants. $2 million available in the 2027 cycle.
- **Timing:** 2027 cycle pre-applications ran July 6 to September 4, 2026; approved full applications are accepted October 7, 2026 through January 7, 2027. Awards in February 2027. If the pre-application window was missed, target the 2028 cycle opening around July 2027.

### 3.5 USDA Rural Development Community Facilities (direct loans, grants, Economic Impact Initiative)
- **Administered by:** USDA Rural Development Louisiana state office, Alexandria. Rolling applications.
- **What it funds:** Community centers, recreation centers, parks equipment, vehicles. Grant share rises with poverty and small population; East Carroll sits in the highest grant tier (up to 75 percent for the lowest-income communities).
- **Action:** Call the area specialist for Northeast Louisiana and ask for a pre-application meeting. USDA will tell you the exact grant/loan blend before you write anything.

### 3.6 Delta Regional Authority, States' Economic Development Assistance Program (SEDAP)
- **Administered by:** DRA through the Louisiana state alternate and the North Delta LDD.
- **What it funds:** Basic public infrastructure, transportation, workforce. A recreation complex is fundable when framed as public infrastructure supporting tourism and quality of life. Distressed parishes get the highest share.
- **Timing:** 2026 cycle closed July 31, 2026. Expect the 2027 NOFA around June 2027.

### 3.7 LDWF Boating and Fishing Access Program (federal Sport Fish Restoration)
- **Administered by:** LDWF, Melissa Longman, mlongman@wlf.la.gov, 225-765-2343.
- **What it funds:** Boat ramps, fishing piers, bank access, parking at those sites. 75 percent federal, 25 percent local match.
- **Timing:** Applications normally accepted June 1 to January 31. A third-party listing says the 2027 cycle was cancelled with the next window January 2028; confirm with LDWF.
- **Action:** Lake Providence has two mapped boat ramps and heavy fishing use. A new ADA fishing pier and ramp upgrade is a strong first application.

### 3.8 Louisiana Capital Outlay
- **Administered by:** Louisiana Division of Administration, Facility Planning and Control.
- **What it funds:** State bond money for local public facilities, including recreation. Requires a legislator sponsor. Requests are due November 1 each year for the following session.
- **Action:** Contact the parish's state representative and senator now about a request for the 2027 session.

### 3.9 Smaller and private programs worth one afternoon each
- **AARP Community Challenge** (quick-action grants, deadline usually early March): benches, trail improvements, fitness equipment for 50-plus residents.
- **T-Mobile Hometown Grants** (up to $50,000, quarterly): the program was reported to be closing out; check mainstreet.org.
- **KABOOM!** playground builds for low-income communities.
- **National Fitness Campaign** outdoor fitness courts (state matching grants exist in some years).
- **Walmart and Entergy local community grants** for Lake Providence-area projects.
- **EPA Brownfields assessment** through LDEQ's statewide assessment grant, if any candidate site is a former industrial or commercial parcel.

## 4. GIS and mapping funding and free resources

The parish does not need a paid GIS to apply for these. QGIS (installed on this machine) is free and the project in `gis/` already covers the parish. The programs below pay for data and mapping capacity.

### 4.1 USGS 3D Elevation Program, Data Collaboration Announcement (lidar)
- FY 2027 round: planning estimates due November 13, 2026; submissions due December 4, 2026. Local governments are eligible as applicants and funding partners. Partner with LSU's Louisiana Geographic Information Center or the state GIS office so the parish is included in a multi-parish lidar collection rather than applying alone.

### 4.2 FEMA Cooperating Technical Partners
- Funds flood mapping work by local partners. Requires a signed partnership agreement with FEMA Region 6 before applying. Parish-wide elevation and flood mapping doubles as site-selection data for parks (Lake Providence frontage is flood-exposed).

### 4.3 FEMA Hazard Mitigation (BRIC, Hazard Mitigation Grant Program)
- Open-space acquisition and nature-based projects (floodable parks, bank stabilization) are eligible mitigation activities. Route through the Governor's Office of Homeland Security and Emergency Preparedness (GOHSEP). Status of BRIC changed in 2025 and 2026; confirm with GOHSEP which program is open.

### 4.4 Esri programs
- Esri has no free local-government grant. The Small Government Enterprise Agreement is a fixed annual fee based on population; for a parish of 7,000 it is the lowest tier. The Esri Small Nonprofit Grant (free ArcGIS for 501(c)(3) organizations with 25 or fewer employees) applies if a "Friends of East Carroll Parks" nonprofit is formed, which is also useful for private fundraising.

### 4.5 Free state data
- LSU Atlas (atlas.ga.lsu.edu) hosts statewide lidar, orthoimagery, boundaries, and hydrography that can be loaded directly into the QGIS project.
- LDWF, Louisiana DOTD, and the Census Bureau provide the roads, water, and demographic layers already in `gis/data/processed/east_carroll.gpkg`.

### 4.6 NPS Rivers, Trails and Conservation Assistance (RTCA)
- Not money, but free professional planning staff for one to two years. They help write the master plan, run public meetings, and produce maps that feed LWCF and RTP applications. Applications accepted roughly January to March each year; contact the NPS Southeast or Intermountain region RTCA office before applying.
